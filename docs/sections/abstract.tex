\begin{abstract}
Evaluating text clustering solutions is inherently challenging due to the subjective nature of human preferences and the absence of a universal ground-truth assignment.
This paper introduces \textit{Cluster Around and Find Out}, an interactive, conversational text clustering system that leverages large language models (LLMs) to elicit user preferences through multi-turn dialogue.
Rather than evaluating the underlying clustering algorithm in isolation, this study focuses on the quality, efficiency, and conversational dynamics of preference elicitation.
We design a controlled experimental framework ($N=108$ runs) simulating distinct user personas and goals to evaluate three interaction paradigms: yes/no, multiple-choice, and open-ended questions. 
Our primary outcome tracks the conversational turns required to achieve convergence, complemented by secondary measures of token efficiency and cluster faithfulness evaluated via an LLM-as-judge. 
We hypothesize that multiple-choice queries offer the optimal tradeoff between human cognitive load and information gain per turn. 
Finally, we compare our interactive approach against an uninstructed baseline to isolate the impact of preference-driven embedding guidance.
\end{abstract}
